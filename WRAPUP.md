# Wrap-up: what this study actually established

_Auto-generated by `build_wrapup.py`. Companion files: `resolution.md`, `CLAIMS_RESOLUTION.md`, `ARCHIVE_MANIFEST.md`, `claims_ledger.csv`._

## TL;DR

- The study is **an effect-estimation study, not a predictor**. It produces controlled local contrasts and negative results; the pooled formula loses to a plain ridge once a whole architecture is held out (family RMSE 0.294 vs 0.282).
- The headline methodological result: **only 25 of 94 documented claims clear their own noise floor.** 8 are unauditable (they depend on variables absent from the DB schema), and most of the rest are model-fit statements, not effects.
- Root cause is **item count, not difficulty**: HumanEval+MBPP give 542 items, so a single-config *capability* effect below **~0.084** cannot be resolved, however many times you rerun it.
- The dataset is **frozen and no longer reproducible end-to-end**: 51/192 model files (26.6%) and 63/2088 raw eval results (3.0%) survive.

## 9. Provenance warnings (read before citing any number)

DB schema is missing nothing essential now: `migrate_db.py` added `run_tag, rep, ctx_size, parallel_slots, seed, protocol, language, bench_family, max_tokens, source_file, completed_at` and **removed the unique index that forbade replicates**.
- Legacy rows still predate those columns (ctx/parallel/seed are NULL), so their `ctx=`/`parallel=` scopes remain unauditable.
- No `seed` column → run-to-run stochasticity was unmeasured until W1.
- Raw artifacts map to rows only by timestamp; legacy hash collisions were previously confirmed, so coefficient slices from old sweeps are lower-confidence.
- Exact-score ties across models are **not** duplication evidence (the k/164 grid produces them; observed ties are *below* the shuffled null).
